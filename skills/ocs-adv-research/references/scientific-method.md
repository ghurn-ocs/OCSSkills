# Scientific method and evidence discipline

## Research protocol

Write the problem as a causal question: in population/context P, does intervention I outperform baseline B on outcome O within constraints C? Distinguish prediction from explanation. List what evidence would change the decision and what result would falsify the favored approach.

Separate feasibility (can it work), validity (does the method establish the claim), utility (does it help the user), viability (can it be delivered sustainably), and safety. Passing one cannot substitute for another.

Search scholarly indexes, original papers, standards and official implementation documentation. Record exact queries, retrieval dates, study populations, versions, inclusion/exclusion rules and contradictory results. Follow cited foundational work where material. Do not count syndicated reports or papers using the same dataset as independent replication. Check corrections/retractions and applicability, not only publication prestige. Refresh mutable facts before relying on them.

For quantitative methods, expose derivations or executable reproductions, parameter sensitivity, unit checks, boundary cases, computational/resource costs and failure regimes. For empirical work, capture protocol, sampling frame, measurement instrument, exclusions, raw-data location, analysis version and reproducibility commands. Keep sensitive raw data out of public deliverables.

## Evidence ledger

Claim status is distinct from evidence strength:

- Established: sufficiently supported foundational result within explicitly stated conditions; still revisable.
- Observed: directly measured in the recorded sample and setting.
- Inferred: reasoned from evidence; expose assumptions and alternative explanations.
- Hypothesized: plausible but untested prediction.
- Unknown: no adequate evidence or conflicting results without resolution.

Rate source strength separately: original standard/primary study/direct observation; credible synthesis; secondary explanation; anecdote/vendor assertion/model suggestion. These are descriptors, not automatic truth rankings. Record relevance, independence, recency, bias, measurement validity and uncertainty. A replicated irrelevant study does not validate the target population. A model response is a search lead, not a verified source.

## Experiment integrity

Predeclare primary outcome, unit of randomization, eligibility, minimum worthwhile effect, sample-size/power assumptions, duration, allocation, exclusions, guardrails and stopping rule. Diagnose instrumentation and sample-ratio mismatch before interpreting effects. Account for clustering, repeated measures, multiple comparisons, contamination, novelty and seasonality. Use an appropriate sequential method if monitoring for early stopping; repeated conventional significance checks inflate false positives.

Use confidence/credible intervals with their assumptions. Absence of a significant difference is not equivalence. Define a non-inferiority margin and adequate sensitivity when protecting existing behavior. Mark post-hoc segment analysis exploratory. Report failed, negative and inconclusive results. If traffic is insufficient, choose observational or qualitative evidence, state causal limits, and retain hold rather than manufacture statistical certainty.

## Adversarial memo

Answer: What is the strongest competing explanation? What evidence contradicts us? Which assumption is least secure? What would a domain expert reject? Could the gain come from selection, leakage, overfitting or measurement changes? Could a simpler process solve the problem? Could buyers decline despite user enthusiasm? Who is disadvantaged? What hidden migration/support costs reverse the decision?

Prioritize the next experiment by expected decision impact, information gain, cost, reversibility and ethical burden. Record why it is preferable to further desk research. Do not promise exhaustive discovery, novelty or legal clearance.
